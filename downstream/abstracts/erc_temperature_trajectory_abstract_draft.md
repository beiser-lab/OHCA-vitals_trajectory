## Submission-Ready Draft

**Purpose of the study:** Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) is a heterogeneous syndrome, and clinically meaningful subphenotypes could improve prognostication and inform personalized post-arrest care. We sought to identify temperature trajectory subphenotypes among ICU-admitted patients with OHCA.

**Materials and methods:** In a pooled multicenter cohort, first ICU-admitted OHCA encounters from 11 sites were included. Temperature measurements during the first 72 hours after the first recorded temperature were reduced to one observation per patient-hour and standardized. A four-class latent class mixed model was fit to these longitudinal temperature data, and the resulting classes were labeled according to trajectory shape as hypothermic, normothermic, rapid decline, and persistent high. Patient characteristics and outcomes were compared across groups. Hospital mortality differences were assessed using chi-square testing and crude risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

**Results:** Of 18,809 encounters, 18,260 (97.1%) were classifiable into four temperature trajectory groups: hypothermic (n = 4,392; 23.4%), normothermic (n = 10,243; 54.5%), rapid decline (n = 445; 2.4%), and persistent high (n = 3,180; 16.9%). The hypothermic group was the oldest and had the highest hospital mortality (77.8%), whereas the persistent-high group was the youngest and had the lowest hospital mortality (35.4%); mortality in the normothermic and rapid-decline groups was 43.0% and 57.5%, respectively. Hospital mortality differed across trajectories (P < 0.001). Compared with the persistent-high group, hypothermic patients had more than twofold higher unadjusted risk of death (RR, 2.20; 95% CI, 2.09-2.31). Similar mortality ordering was observed across sites.

**Conclusions:** Four temperature trajectory subphenotypes were identified in ICU-admitted patients with OHCA and were strongly associated with hospital mortality. Temperature trajectories may support early risk stratification and motivate future studies of phenotype-guided temperature management.

**Keywords:** OHCA; cardiac arrest; temperature; trajectories; subphenotypes
